How today works

Part B (with your group): Near the end of the period, you'll meet with everyone else who read Singh. You'll share your answers, compare where you agree and disagree, and plan for tomorrow's fishbowl.

Part A

On your own

About 55 minutes. Don't rush. Your thinking now is what you'll bring to your group.

A1

First take

5 min
A1.1

Singh chose the word "apartheid" for his title. What was your reaction to that word before you read? After?

A1.2

What's one thing in the article that confused you, surprised you, or annoyed you?

You'll come back to this with your group.

A2

Map the argument

15 min

Before you can agree or disagree with Singh, make sure you know what he actually says. Use the text, not memory.

A2.1

Write Singh's thesis in one sentence, in your own words.

Keep in mind who he is: a senior adviser to India's Prime Minister, writing just months after India's May 1998 tests.

A2.2

The article has four sections. What is the main point of each?

SectionMain point
"The Tests of May" pp. 42-44
"The Failure of the Old Regime" pp. 44-46
"A Fine Balance" pp. 46-49
"Join the Club" pp. 49-52
A2.3

Singh gives several reasons India "had no choice" but to test. Find them. What evidence or events does he point to for each?

ReasonEvidence / event + page
A2.4 pp. 49-52

Now that India has the bomb, what does Singh promise India will and won't do?

A3

Dig into the guiding questions

20 min

These are the questions from your reading guide. For each one, find at least one line in the text that supports your answer and note the page.

A3.1

What does Singh mean by "nuclear apartheid"?

Find where he actually uses the phrase. What is he comparing the nuclear system to, and why would he choose such a loaded word?

A3.2

How does Singh connect nuclear policy to ideas of sovereignty and dignity for countries in the Global South?

On p. 49 he says being a nuclear weapons state is not "a status for others to grant." Who does he think gets to decide? Why does that matter so much to him?

A3.3

What global double standards does he highlight?

Look at the questions he fires off on p. 43, and at the "club" he describes on p. 48. Who is inside, who is outside, and who makes the rules?

A3.4

How does this article relate to ideas of postcolonialism, inequality, and sovereignty?

India became independent in 1947 and chose nonalignment instead of joining either Cold War bloc. How might that history shape the way Singh sees rules written by the world's most powerful countries?

★ BIG QUESTION

Should all countries have an equal right to possess nuclear weapons?

First answer as Singh would. Then answer for yourself.

Singh offers the world two options. What are they? Which one does he seem to really want?

A4

Test the argument

10 min

Understanding Singh doesn't mean agreeing with him. Pick at least two of these.

A4.1

Singh says India is committed to getting rid of nuclear weapons, in an article defending India's nuclear tests. Is that a contradiction? How does he try to make it fit?

A4.2

Who is Singh, and who is he writing for? How should that change the way you read his argument?

Is this analysis, persuasion, or both?

A4.3

Singh says the tests "were not directed against any country" and that India "shall not engage in an arms race." Pakistan tested two weeks later. What do you make of that?

A4.4

If we accept Singh's logic for India, who else could use the same argument? Does that strengthen or weaken his case?

A5

Since 1998

5 min

Singh wrote this more than 25 years ago. A lot has happened in South Asia and with the global nuclear rules since.

A5.1

What do you know (or can you quickly find) about what's happened since 1998 between India and Pakistan, or between India and the rest of the world on nuclear issues? Do those events make Singh look right, wrong, or neither?

Part B

With your group

About 20 minutes. Bring your answers from Part A. Everyone shares before anyone debates.

B

Share and compare

20 min
B1

Go around and read your thesis sentences (A2.1) out loud. How are they different? Which one comes closest?

B2

Compare your answers to the Big Question. Where does your group agree? Where do you split?

B3

Share your confusions from A1.2 and your critiques from A4. Can the group clear any of them up? Which ones are still open?

B4

Did anyone in your group change your mind or make you see something you missed? What was it?
